Reefer Container

Scaling beyond single machines — containerized HPC infrastructure for large-scale computational campaigns

The Reefer Container project represents the next generation of the PS3 computing initiative, scaling from individual campus resources to a containerized, transportable high-performance computing facility. This project demonstrates innovative approaches to HPC infrastructure design and deployment.

Container-Based HPC Infrastructure

GK

The Reefer Container integrates multiple computing nodes into a transportable shipping container, complete with power distribution, cooling, and networking infrastructure. This approach allows researchers to deploy significant computational resources at remote sites or collaborate across institutions.

The container design prioritizes modularity, allowing for easy upgrades and reconfiguration as technology evolves, and incorporates redundancy for reliability in research computing environments.

Power & Cooling Systems

GK

A key innovation of the Reefer Container is its integrated power delivery and cooling systems. These systems manage heat dissipation from dozens of computing nodes while maintaining optimal operational temperatures in a confined space.

The power infrastructure includes UPS capability and automated failover mechanisms, ensuring uninterrupted operation during power anomalies.

Networking & Interconnect

GK

High-bandwidth interconnects within the container and to external networks are critical for performance. We implemented Gigabit Ethernet and specialized high-speed fabrics to minimize communication overhead in parallel computations.

Large-Scale Gravitational Wave Campaigns

GK, SF

The Reefer Container enabled large-scale parameter studies and survey computations that would be impractical on traditional shared HPC facilities. Researchers could explore vast parameter spaces of black hole configurations and orbital dynamics.

Transportable Research Infrastructure

GK

By containerizing the facility, it becomes possible to transport significant computational resources to collaborating institutions, national laboratories, or international venues. This flexibility has enabled collaborative research and training across geographic boundaries.

Lessons for Cloud & Distributed Computing

GK

The Reefer Container project provides practical experience with containerized, modular computing infrastructure that informed later transitions to cloud computing and distributed HPC environments. Many of the architectural principles apply to modern cloud deployments.
