Scientific Computation

High-performance computing, numerical methods, and computational physics for gravitational phenomena

This section covers our computational research in gravitational physics, including numerical relativity, high-performance computing techniques, and algorithm development for solving Einstein's equations.

Time-Domain Teukolsky Equation Solvers

GK, SF, RP

We develop and maintain high-order, accurate numerical solvers for the Teukolsky equation governing perturbations around Kerr black holes. These solvers use finite-difference methods with adaptive mesh refinement to accurately track gravitational wave generation.

Our codes are implemented in Fortran and C++ with OpenMP parallelization for modern multi-core processors, achieving both accuracy and efficiency in long-term evolutions required for ringdown analysis.

GPU-Accelerated Numerical Codes

GK

We leverage GPU computing via CUDA and OpenCL to accelerate time-domain simulations. This allows us to run larger domain sizes and longer evolution times while maintaining accuracy.

Our GPU implementations achieve 10-100× speedups over CPU-only versions, enabling parameter studies and uncertainty quantification that would be prohibitively expensive on traditional HPC resources.

Surrogate Model Development

SF, GK

Building on extensive numerical simulations, we develop reduced-order models (surrogates) that can quickly predict gravitational waveforms for new parameters without running expensive simulations.

These surrogates use machine learning techniques to efficiently interpolate the high-dimensional parameter space of black hole configurations and EMRI trajectories.

Validation & Convergence Testing

GK, RP, LB

Rigorous numerical validation is critical to ensuring our results are trustworthy. We perform extensive convergence tests, comparing different discretization schemes and grid resolutions to establish error estimates.

We also compare with analytic results where available and cross-validate with other groups' codes through collaborative benchmarking efforts.

Algorithm Development for EMRIs

GK, SF

Extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs) require specialized computational techniques due to the vast scale separation between the massive and stellar-mass black holes. We develop specialized algorithms and multiscale methods to handle these challenges.

Open-Source Software & Public Codes

GK, SF

We contribute to open-source efforts in computational relativity, sharing our codes and methodologies with the broader community to advance gravitational wave science and computational physics research globally.
